- **Verdict:** UNEXPLAINED

## Rationale
The flagged signal is a declared candidate holding a position in a Kalshi market resolving on his own candidacy — an outcome he can directly influence. Section 4 affirmatively records no public directional catalyst (no polling shock, dropout, endorsement, or scandal) in the days before the cutoff. The only public footprint (Section 2) is the candidate publicly depicting his own trading, which is the conduct under investigation, not an exogenous information catalyst that would explain ordinary trading. Rule 5.17(z) (Section 3) frames the position as an insider/control situation whose direction and concentration derive from the actor's private influence over the event, which public information cannot account for. Thin-market activity explains ambient volume, not the self-interested directional position.

```
The flagged signal here is a candidate holding positions in a Kalshi market tied to his own candidacy — a market whose outcome he can directly influence as a participant in the resolving event.

Let me work through what the dossier offers as potential public explanation:

**Section 1** — Confirms it's a thin market with low open interest, sparse trading, wide spreads. Nothing here supplies a directional catalyst.

**Section 2** — The candidate publicly posted videos depicting/discussing his own trading. This makes the *behavior* public knowledge, but it does not constitute a public *information catalyst* explaining ordinary trading on public info. A candidate's self-promotion of his trading is not exogenous public information that would drive a directional position; it's the very conduct under investigation.

**Section 3** — Rule 5.17(z) explicitly prohibits trading on outcomes one controls or helps decide. This is context that *frames* the conduct as problematic, not context that explains it as ordinary.

**Section 4** — Critically: "No major public polling shock, dropout, endorsement, or scandal specific to this race is in the public record in the days before the cutoff." Ordinary campaign filings and social-media campaigning existed, but there is **no public catalyst** — no news, no data release, no odds move, no scheduled event — that would account for a directional, concentrated position in this market.

The core issue: the flagged pattern is not merely "trading in a thin election market." It's a self-interested participant holding a position in a market whose resolution he personally influences. Even if surrounding low-volume activity is unremarkable, there is nothing public that explains the *direction and concentration* of the candidate's own position. A candidate has private, non-public influence over the outcome (his own campaign decisions, whether he stays in, effort exerted), which is exactly the informational/control asymmetry Rule 5.17(z) targets. No public information can substitute for or "explain away" that.

Given the dossier explicitly notes the absence of any polling shock, dropout, endorsement, scandal, or other directional catalyst in the days before the cutoff, there is no public basis to characterize this as ordinary trading on public information. The behavior is driven by the actor's own participation in the resolving event — precisely the thing public information cannot account for.

This is not weak or partial evidence pointing toward an explanation; the dossier affirmatively documents the *absence* of a directional public catalyst. So this is not merely AMBIGUOUS — the flagged pattern is unaccounted-for by public information.
```
